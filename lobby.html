FERRET FRENZY FIELD GUIDE · Revision 3.3

How to Play

Welcome to Ferret Frenzy: the game where the Minnow Treats have vanished, everyone was supposedly asleep, and at least one ferret is about to give a very suspicious account of their bedtime.

You’ll get a secret Starting Role, wake at private times during twelve Burrow Hours, and collect little pieces of evidence: whether the treats were still there, who else was awake, and what your role let you learn. Then morning comes, everybody explains themselves, and the group locks one simultaneous Paw Point vote.

The big question is simple: Who was the historical Raider who stole the treats? The hard part is that cards can move, people can change sides, and a true story from Hour 3 might not describe what’s true at dawn.

So compare your evidence, question everyone’s timeline, and please remember: being caught in a lie is bad. Being caught by a ferret with a paw-shaped voting system is worse. Let’s open the burrow!

The goal

Ferret Frenzy is a social deduction game. Players privately gather clues during the night, then use public discussion and one simultaneous vote to figure out who stole the Minnow Treats.

  • Business wins if the historical Raider ends up caught through the final catch rules.
  • Bandit side wins if the historical Raider escapes.
Catching a Bandit accomplice or a converted Hunter by itself does not win for Business. The central mystery is always who actually stole the treats.

Setup and the cards

Everyone gets one secretly dealt Starting Card. Three additional cards are left face down in the Hammock. The player-count recipe chooses which cards go into the round; the table lists the set before dealing, so three cards remain in the Hammock. Solo + Bots fills the game to four seats by default, and bots receive roles from the same random deal as human players.

Keep three ideas separate:
  • Starting Role: your original role. It controls your night actions and never changes.
  • Current Card: a card that may move during the night.
  • Current Allegiance: your side at a particular time. Card movement or conversion can change it.
A moved card does not give you another role’s night action or rewrite what you did earlier.

For allegiance, the historical Raider stays Bandit-side. Trouble who becomes In On It and a converted Hunter also stay Bandit-side. Otherwise, a Bandit Current Card means Bandit-side and a non-Bandit Current Card means Business-side. Receiving a Bandit card does not reveal the starting Bandit roster or make someone the historical Raider.

The eight roles

BanditSable / Black Sable

Starting Bandits know the starting Bandit roster. The earliest-waking starting Bandit becomes the historical Raider and steals the treats during that wake. If starting Bandits tie for earliest, the tied players privately roll d6; the lowest unique roll wins. Tied lowest rollers reroll until one has a unique lowest result. Other Bandits try to keep the Raider from being caught.

DookerSilver Mitt

Wakes three times at distinct Burrow Hours: first to observe, then to Glimpse one player’s current allegiance at that moment, then to view one Hammock card and optionally Trip it onto a player without seeing the displaced card. A Glimpse can become stale later.

ItchyCinnamon

Always gets one normal wake. A private d6 roll of 5–6 activates a second distinct wake. After the theft, an activated Itchy gets a two-player clue containing the historical Raider and, when possible, one non-Raider decoy. The clue never says which name is the Raider. If no legal pair can be formed, the clue is marked degraded.

TroubleChampagne

Blindly swaps the Current Cards of two different other players and does not see the cards being swapped. If Trouble wakes in the exact theft window, Trouble becomes In On It before the swap and learns who the historical Raider is.

BusinessWarm Sable Roan

Has one ordinary wake. If Business wakes alone, they can choose another player and inspect just one randomly selected wake result from that player. They never see the target’s whole schedule.

SnugglerChocolate

Before Hour 1, bonds two different other players. The bonded partners learn each other’s identities, but not either person’s role or allegiance. If one partner is caught, the other can be marked Bond-Revealed in the results. That reveal is not itself a catch.

HunterBlaze

Recognizes the starting Guardian before Hour 1 and wakes twice. At the first wake that overlaps with a starting Bandit, Hunter privately rolls d6 once: 1–2 converts Hunter to Bandit side; 3–6 leaves Hunter loyal. If still loyal, Hunter places one Hunt Mark after the later wake. If a loyal Hunter is caught, the marked target is also caught only if Bandit-aligned when the mark resolves. A converted Hunter has no loyal Hunt Mark effect.

GuardianDark-Eyed White

Recognizes the starting Hunter, but never learns whether Hunter later converts. Just before Paw Point votes lock, Guardian may protect one other player from Paw Point capture for that resolution. Protection does not shield against other effects.

Night: twelve Burrow Hours

There are two dice. The d12 selects private Burrow Hours. The d6 is used for Itchy’s activation, Hunter’s conversion check, and a tied earliest-Bandit Raider selection. 1 selects d6; 2 selects d12. A deliberate joystick flick commits the required roll. Raw dice results are private. Multi-wake roles get distinct hours; if a player rolls the same hour twice, only the duplicate is rerolled.

  1. Scheduled players wake and see who else woke with them.
  2. They see the Minnow Treat state before actions in that window.
  3. If this is the Raider’s theft hour, the Raider taps the Minnow Treat hotspot in their private scene. The state changes from PRESENT to MISSING.
  4. Witness and conversion effects resolve, followed by scheduled Starting-Role actions.
  5. Facts are committed privately; players return to sleep until their next wake.

The sleep screen stays inside the game viewport, with the shell and Burrow Clock still visible. Private role prompts and facts are for you; other players do not automatically see them.

PRESENT and MISSING help bracket when the theft happened, but neither state identifies the thief by itself. Compare the exact hours and co-wakers to narrow the timeline.

Morning Business: compare evidence

After Hour 12, night actions stop and the group discusses what happened. The same shared chat stays available through voting. The public channels #burrow, #evidence, #claims, #paw-point, and #narrator organize the conversation; they are not secret faction rooms. Turn Mode can be enabled for Morning Business and Paw Point: players Raise Paw, the host gives someone the floor, and that player speaks until yielding.

A useful testimony order is Hour → Treat State → Co-wakers → Role fact → Deduction. For example: “I woke at Hour 5, the treats were PRESENT, and I woke with Jules.” That is a claim the group can compare against other timelines.

The app does not verify public claims against hidden roles. Quick Role Claim helps you write a claim, but truthful claims and bluffs use the same tool. Starting Role and Current Card are different, so be clear about which one you mean.

  • Fact: something the game privately showed you, such as your wake hour or a legal role result.
  • Claim: something a player says publicly. It might be true, mistaken, selective, or false.
  • Inference: a deduction from facts and claims. Confidence does not turn it into proof.

A fact can also be true at one time and stale later. For instance, Dooker might have seen someone as Business-aligned before that player was converted.

Paw Point and the results

Immediately before voting, Guardian may privately choose one other player to protect. Then every active player locks one private vote for another player. You cannot vote for yourself, and votes lock simultaneously.

After protection is applied, the game catches every unprotected player tied for the highest eligible vote total. If protection leaves no positive eligible vote total, Paw Point catches nobody. There is no host tiebreak vote.

  1. The Paw Point catch set resolves.
  2. A valid loyal Hunter Hunt Mark can add a catch.
  3. Snuggle Bond result reveals resolve.
  4. The game checks whether the historical Raider was caught.
Bond-Revealed is not the same as Caught. It can expose a bonded partner’s result information, but it is not an extra capture and cannot substitute for catching the Raider.

Business wins only if the historical Raider is caught through the final catch rules. Bandit side wins if the Raider escapes. Catching only an accomplice or converted Hunter is not enough.

Quick controls

  • /1/ selects d6; /2/ selects d12.
  • A deliberate joystick flick rolls the selected die.
  • A confirms or selects; B goes back or cancels before locking; X triggers the context-sensitive role action.
  • Private Info and Notebook are in the HUD/menu.

Narrator, Solo + Bots, and privacy

The Narrator can announce shared phases and public cues. Private role instructions and private facts stay private, even when the Narrator speaks. Solo + Bots fills open seats and gives each bot its dealt role; bots reason from authorized facts and public claims rather than hidden game truth.

Raw d6/d12 results, hidden cards, secret conversions, Hammock views, and private action prompts stay on authorized views. Reconnection restores only what that player may know. Post-game Trail of Trouble may explain hidden events when result/replay data allows, but replay never changes what was legal to know during play.

First-round advice

Write down your own hours, Treat States, and co-wakers. Share the facts you choose to share, listen for timelines that agree or conflict, and separate what you personally saw from what you suspect. Then vote for the player you think is the historical Raider.
